## Why an advantage is plausible; why it is not demonstrated

The proposed chain is: a real enforceable commitment changes expected future cash flows; interpreting the original document and its significance takes time; unusually strong opening participation followed by a held low and renewed buying could indicate remaining demand; that repricing could persist over the frozen multi-session horizon. The reclaim is a testable timing condition, not itself an explanation of value. A material agreement could already be anticipated, low-margin, costly, cancellable or offset by financing/dilution. Ten percent of equity capitalization in contract revenue does not imply ten percent upside or any positive net-present-value change. No surprise/earnings estimate, margin assumption or new financial filter is introduced to rescue A.

For N whole shares, A buys at a=ask×1.0025 and sells at b=bid×0.9975. Net dollars=N×(b−a)−6, with N=floor((1000−3)/a); report return against the full USD1,000 including idle cash. Thus N×(b−a) must exceed USD6 before net profit, and both comparison excesses must also be positive. At roughly USD1,000 deployed, commissions alone consume about0.6%; slippage adds roughly0.5%, plus the spread already embedded in ask/bid. Near the allowed3% spread ceiling, the hurdle can be several percent. These are arithmetic hurdles, not expected returns. A displayed quote supports a conservative capacity model only; it never proves an order would fill. Gaps and halts can make a next-open exit unavailable or much worse than an invalidation level.

There is no retained authentic A performance series demonstrating the mechanism or an after-cost advantage.** Synthetic positive/negative cases establish software behavior only.
